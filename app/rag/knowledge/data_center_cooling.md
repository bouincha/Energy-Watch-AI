# Data Center Cooling and PUE

## Power Usage Effectiveness
PUE = total facility energy / IT equipment energy. A PUE of 1.0 is ideal. Modern efficient
facilities reach 1.2 to 1.4; legacy rooms are often above 1.6. Cooling is the largest
contributor to the overhead, followed by UPS and distribution losses.

## PUE drift
A progressive increase of cooling consumption while the IT load is stable (PUE drift)
usually indicates: fouled filters or coils on CRAH/CRAC units, a chilled-water setpoint
lowered by mistake, failing fans, refrigerant problems, or hot/cold aisle mixing caused
by missing blanking panels or open containment doors.
Corrective action: inspect and clean CRAH filters and coils, verify supply air and chilled
water setpoints, check containment, and compare per-unit power to find the faulty unit.

## Raise temperatures
ASHRAE recommends an inlet temperature of 18 to 27 °C for IT equipment. Raising the supply
air temperature and the chilled water temperature enables more free-cooling hours and
saves 2 to 4 % of cooling energy per °C.

## Airflow management
Use hot-aisle or cold-aisle containment, install blanking panels, seal cable cut-outs, and
match fan speed to the IT load with variable-speed drives.
