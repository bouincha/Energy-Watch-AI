# Compressed Air Systems

## Why compressed air is expensive
Compressed air is one of the most expensive utilities in a factory: only about 10 to 15 %
of the electrical energy used by a compressor is converted into useful work. Industrial
sites typically use 10 to 30 % of their electricity for compressed air.

## Leaks
Leaks commonly waste 20 to 30 % of the compressor output. A leak shows up in metering as a
sustained increase of compressor consumption, visible also at night and during weekends
when production is stopped (the "leak load"). A 3 mm leak at 7 bar wastes roughly
2 to 3 kW continuously.
Corrective action: run an ultrasonic leak-detection survey, tag and repair leaks
(couplings, hoses, filters, regulators, drains), and repeat the survey every quarter.
Measure the leak load during a non-production period: compressor power with all
equipment idle should be below 10 % of full-load power.

## Pressure optimization
Every 1 bar reduction of the generation pressure saves about 7 % of compressor energy.
Avoid using a high pressure for the whole network when only one machine needs it.

## Shutdown outside production
Isolate the network with an automatic valve and stop compressors when production lines
are off. Install a sequencer to run the minimum number of compressors at full load.

## Heat recovery
Up to 90 % of compressor input power can be recovered as heat for hot water or space
heating.
